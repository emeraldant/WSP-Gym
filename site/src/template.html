WSP-Gym · weight-space repair

We trained an open model to repair neural networks by reading their raw weights

Inkling 2 Small, post-trained with LoRA on Tinker, reads a broken network's weights as plain text and answers with a small edit that fixes it. No tools, no forward passes and, after training, no reasoning.

{{byline}} · November 2026 · code, frozen test sets and every rollout log

TL;DR

Weight-space programming, a language model reading a network's weights and writing better values with no gradients, had only been shown with closed frontier models used off the shelf. We trained an open-weights model to do it on one task: repairing small ReLU networks that each contain one planted fault.

Untrained, Inkling 2 Small fixes {{think_id}} of held-out broken networks, and only by reasoning for about {{think_tokens}} tokens per network. After supervised training and RL on Tinker it fixes {{rl_id}} {{rl_id_ci}} in about {{rl_tokens}} tokens, with no reasoning: {{vs_think}} points {{vs_think_ci}}, paired on the same {{vs_think_n}} networks.

Off the shelf, the {{offshelf_n_once_open}} other open-weights models that would answer at once fix at most {{offshelf_best_open_direct}} of a sample of the same networks{{offshelf_tldr_reason}}. GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol fix {{frontier_range}}. The networks are tiny, and most of the gain comes from the supervised stage.

The task

Setup A small ReLU network that should compute a one-line function contains at most one planted fault. The model sees the specification, the architecture and every weight as text, and replies with at most 3 edit operations, each touching one row, one column or one scalar.

An edit is scored only by how the edited network behaves: recovery = (acc_after - acc_faulty) / (acc_clean - acc_faulty), and a network counts as repaired at a recovery of {{repair_threshold}} or more. Any equivalent fix counts. About one network in six is healthy, and the right answer there is the empty edit [].

How it was trained on Tinker

Setup Rank-{{rank}} LoRA on Inkling 2 Small. Tinker runs the forward and backward passes; the gym and the reward run locally. Checkpoint chosen on validation networks.

  1. Supervised, {{sft_steps}} steps × {{batch}} networks ({{sft_nets}} networks, half of them trained by gradient descent rather than compiled by hand). The target is the inverse of the planted fault.
  2. RL, {{rl_steps}} steps with the tinker-cookbook policy-gradient loop: {{rl_groups}} networks per step, {{rl_group}} samples of each, rewards centred within each group. The reward is the behavioural score: +1 for [] on a healthy network, a penalty for editing one, -1 for an invalid answer.

What did not work

  • Pure RL on the repair task. From the base model, RL learned the answer format in one or two steps and then collapsed to always answering "no edit": when almost every guess fails, doing nothing scores better. Hence the supervised stage.
  • Real tabular data. On networks under-trained on 8 public datasets the proposer never saw, one trained proposal removes {{real_rl1}} of errors against {{real_rand1}} for a random edit, but with eight tries the edge mostly disappears: {{real_rl8}} {{real_rl8_ci}} against {{real_rand8}} {{real_rand8_ci}}. Gradient descent removes {{real_grad}}.
  • Public image classifiers. Editing the final layer of {{ih_n}} classifiers from the Hugging Face Hub improved none of the well-trained ones; the only clear gain ({{ih_best_change}} points) was on a broken model that scores {{ih_best_before}}.

    What this does not show

    • Anything about large networks: these have tens to hundreds of weights, one planted fault each, and compute functions with a one-line formula.
    • That RL did most of the work. Most of the gain comes from supervised training on faults we planted, so the fix is known. RL adds about {{rl_gain_pooled_0}} points ({{rl_gain_pooled}} {{rl_gain_pooled_ci}} pooled over the six test sets; {{rl_gain_id}} in-distribution, within noise), most visibly as fewer edits to healthy networks.
    • Transfer to new kinds of fault: fault types never seen in training stay at {{rl_faults_0}}, and networks from an optimiser it never saw drop to {{rl_external_0}}.
    • Parity with frontier models: GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol fix {{frontier_range}} of the same networks.
    • Robustness across training runs: the final model is one training seed, and the reasoning reference is one effort setting with a {{think_cap_tokens}}-token cap.
    • Better reasoning or real-model gains: asked to reason, the trained model still answers in a few dozen tokens, and editing the final layer of {{ih_n}} public image classifiers improved none of the well-trained ones. {{limits_llm}}

    Method details

    • Test sets: {{n_test}} networks each, generated before training from seeds disjoint from training and validation. Intervals are 95% bootstrap intervals over networks; differences are paired on the same networks.
    • Sampling: no reasoning, temperature {{rl_temp}}, {{rl_max_tokens}}-token cap, one answer per network. Untrained reasoning reference: effort {{think_effort}}, {{think_cap_tokens}}-token cap.
    • The checkpoint was chosen on validation networks (300 hand-built, 200 gradient-made), never on test.
    • Other models were run through OpenRouter on the same prompts, on the first {{offshelf_n}} test networks. Where an API does not allow reasoning to be switched off, its lowest setting was used. An answer cut off at the token limit counts as not repaired for every model.
    • Costs are estimated from token counts at list prices: supervised {{cost_sft}}, RL {{cost_rl}}, evaluation {{cost_eval}}.
