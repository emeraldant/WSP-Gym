**Original query**: How identifiable is the repair from the weights? For a random subsample (~150 faulty + ~40 clean) of data/test_id.jsonl and ~100 faulty of data/test_trained.jsonl, enumerate the single-op edit space used by weightgym/baselines.py (single_op_candidates) and apply each with forward passes to measure: (a) multiplicity of distinct single-op repairs (recovery >= 0.95); (b) how often the planted inverse is the only repair; (c) for clean networks, how many single-op edits leave accuracy unchanged vs break it. Relate multiplicity to the final model's success (runs/final/sft_rl/<split>.jsonl, join on seed) and fault type. Does the model succeed more when many edits work, or does it mostly hit the planted inverse? When it succeeds, how often is its edit the planted inverse vs a functionally equivalent alternative?

**Methodology**
- Sampling (seed 20260): test_id 150 faulty + 40 clean; test_trained 100 faulty (all drawn without replacement). Final model = runs/final/sft_rl (one sample per network, direct, no reasoning), joined on seed (290/290 matched, no duplicates).
- Edit spaces, each candidate applied alone with `apply_edit`, scored on the episode's scoring inputs (`task.accuracy`); recovery = (acc_after-acc_faulty)/(acc_clean-acc_faulty), repair iff >=0.95:
  - BASE = `single_op_candidates` exactly (row/col sign flips on every layer, col swaps within hidden layers). Mean ~54 candidates/net.
  - EXT (my addition, not in baselines.py) = scale row/col by {0.5,2,0.4,2.5,3}, zero any single weight, zero or negate a bias, output-row swaps (to give the non-flip/swap fault types a chance of having an in-space repair).
- Important structural fact: BASE can contain the planted inverse only for sign_flip_in, sign_flip_out, swap_out_cols. For bias_shift, scale_out_col, weight_corrupt, label_swap (2-op) the planted inverse is outside BASE, so BASE alone finds no repair for 36% (test_id) / 38% (trained) of faulty networks. Hence I report multiplicity as "distinct repairing edits among BASE + planted inverse" (`n_rep_base_plus_inv`, >=1 always; sanity: the planted inverse achieves recovery>=0.95 on 250/250) as the primary measure, and "BASE+EXT+inverse" (`n_rep_all`) as a sensitivity. Edits are distinct as literal ops; many repairs can be the same function (e.g. flips of dead neurons), and EXT factor variants (2.5 vs 3.0) are counted as separate edits, so EXT multiplicity is inflated.
- Model "restores planted inverse" = the edited shown-network equals ep.clean weights (allclose 1e-6). "Alternative" = succeeds but yields different weights. Because this is strict (a different value at the same location counts as alternative), I also computed whether the model's op locations (tensor/axis/index) equal the planted edit's (over all faulty episodes in the split, not just the subsample).
- CIs: percentile bootstrap over networks, 2000 resamples. Within-fault-type association: correlation of log-multiplicity with success after demeaning per fault type, permutation p (2000 perms).
- Scripts: discovery/experiments/scripts/R0Q2_enum.py, R0Q2_analyze.py, R0Q2_loc.py; outputs in discovery/experiments/outputs/R0Q2/ (enum.csv, merged.csv, summary.txt); figure discovery/reports/figures/R0Q2_multiplicity.png.

**Interpretation**
- Repair is mostly identifiable (single planted inverse) but not strictly: ~37% (test_id) / 25% (trained) of faulty nets admit another single-op BASE repair, and about 60% / 50% admit one in a broader single-op space. Functional slack in healthy nets is large (24% of flip/swap edits are free), which is the mechanism behind alternative repairs.
- The model's success does NOT depend on multiplicity (null; test_id slope ~0, perm p=0.59, and no monotone trend across bins). It is therefore not succeeding because "many edits work"; it is succeeding by locating the planted fault. Consistent with this, ~85% of its successful edits land on the planted location. Differences in success are driven by fault type (bias_shift/scale/flip easy; swap_out_cols and weight_corrupt harder), not multiplicity.
- Roughly half of test_id successes are not weight-exact restorations, but this is mostly value slack at the right location (e.g. a bias or scale factor that works though differs from the clean value), so "functionally equivalent alternative" mostly means a different value, only ~15% a different location. Exact-clean restoration is rare for weight_corrupt/bias_shift/scale_out_col because the model has to guess a numeric value; the 0.95 recovery threshold is forgiving of that. On test_trained the 15 successes are mostly bias_shift with a non-clean value (small n).
- Caveats: n=40 clean, n=4 label_swap, n=15 trained successes (wide CIs); multiplicity counts literal ops, not distinct functions; BASE cannot express 4 of 7 fault inverses so I added planted-inverse and an ad hoc EXT space (my own choice; EXT counts vary with the factor grid); recovery is computed on the finite scoring inputs (as the gym does), which can make accidental repairs look valid with small input sets (n_inputs as low as 32); the model has one sample per network; location-match figures use all faulty episodes, not the subsample. Only single-op alternatives are counted; the model may also use 2-3-op edits, but invalid/multi-op use is not analyzed here.
- Follow-ups: cluster repairs by induced function (compare outputs on the full input grid) to count truly distinct functions; test whether the model's choice among multiple repairs is predictable by minimal-weight-change; evaluate on held-out inputs to see whether alternative repairs generalize as well as the inverse.
