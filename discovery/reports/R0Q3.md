**Original query**: Cross-policy item difficulty on test_id. Build a network x policy success matrix from every runs/final/<policy>/test_id.jsonl (frontier_* and open_* cover only the first 100 networks; trained checkpoints, untrained, untrained_thinking and baseline_* cover all 500; average multiple samples; cut-off answers = failures). Fit a 1-parameter IRT/Rasch model for network difficulty and policy ability on the first-100 subset. Is difficulty one-dimensional, or do the trained model, reasoning models and forward-pass search fail on different networks (residual correlations, PCA)? Which network properties (family, fault, variant, n_weights, acc drop) explain difficulty? Are there networks no policy repairs, and what are they? Is the trained Inkling's error profile closer to the frontier models' or to the search baseline's?

**Methodology**
- Data: 46 `runs/final/*/test_id.jsonl`; 43 policies after dropping the 3 `*_greedy` duplicates of sampled checkpoints (sft_rl_greedy, scale_rl_greedy, sft_mixed_greedy). Every policy has exactly 1 sample per network (so "averaging" is a no-op). `success` is already False for every cut-off (stop_reason length/malformed) answer, verified (0 cut-off successes), so no re-scoring. Episode metadata (family, fault, variant, acc_clean-acc_faulty = "drop") from `data/test_id.jsonl`; n_weights from rollouts. 78/500 networks are clean controls (12 in the first 100; success there = answering no-op/neutral).
- baseline_* rollouts lack seed 3000000 (499 rows); handled as missing in the Rasch fit, and that network is dropped from the group-residual analyses (first-100 faulty analyses use n=87).
- Rasch: logit P = theta_policy - b_network, ridge L2 (lambda 0.5 on each parameter, i.e. a random-effects-like shrinkage so floor/ceiling policies and all-solved networks are finite), alternating Newton, items = first 100 networks (12 clean + 88 faulty), 43 policies. Item analyses also repeated on all 500 networks using the 12 policies with full coverage (5 checkpoints, untrained, untrained_thinking, 5 baselines).
- Dimensionality: (a) PCA of Rasch standardized residuals (30 policies with 3%<success<97% and no missing cells) vs a parametric-bootstrap null (300 simulations from the fitted Rasch, refit each time); (b) 5-fold cell-held-out log-loss, Rasch vs ridge logistic matrix factorization of rank 1-3 (3 seeds); (c) correlations of group-mean Rasch residuals over faulty-only first-100 networks (Rasch refit on faulty only), bootstrap 95% CI over networks (2000 resamples). Groups: TRAINED (sft_rl, scale_rl, scale_sft, sft, sft_mixed), FRONTIER_STRONG (astra_reasoning, sol_reasoning, grok_reasoning, gemini_reasoning, grok_direct, astra_direct, sol_direct: 92-100% success), OPEN_REASONING (9 open_*_reasoning), FRONTIER_DIRECT_WEAK (6 direct models, 5-46%), SEARCH (baseline_search), GRADIENT, UNTRAINED_THINK, INKLING_FINAL (sft_rl alone). Caveat: residuals from one Rasch fit sum to ~0 per network, which biases cross-group correlations negative (roughly -0.05 to -0.15 for these group sizes), so only large negatives are interpretable.
- Item properties: OLS of Rasch difficulty b on family, fault (clean = a level), variant, standardized n_weights and drop; R2, adjusted R2, 10-fold CV R2 (ridge), bootstrap CI (500) of adjusted R2.
- Scripts: discovery/experiments/scripts/R0Q3_{load,main,groups,extra}.py; outputs in discovery/experiments/outputs/R0Q3/ (logs main.log, groups.log).

**Interpretation**
- Difficulty is not one-dimensional but is dominated by one general factor plus a policy-type axis. The strongest evidence: a significant second residual structure that beats Rasch out of sample, and a CI-excluding-zero negative residual correlation between trained checkpoints and open reasoning models (-0.53), and between Inkling and the strong frontier models (-0.30). The trained model and reasoners each fail where the other succeeds, most visibly on sign_flip_in (trained 20% fail vs open reasoners 74%).
- Inkling vs frontier vs search: neither. Inkling's failure set is statistically unrelated to the search baseline's (phi -0.03 to -0.07, residual r -0.13 to -0.31, i.e. complementary) and also unrelated to the strong frontier models' rare failures (phi -0.02). The closest relatives are its own earlier checkpoints (+0.2 to +0.55) and, weakly, the untrained base model with thinking (phi +0.20, residual r -0.13/-0.17, inconsistent), suggesting its errors are partly checkpoint/model-specific (e.g. swap_out_cols, count). Search is fully deterministic-by-fault-type (solves single-op sign-flip/swap faults, fails bias/label/corrupt), a mechanistic profile unlike any LLM; that complementarity (search 0% fail on swap_out_cols where Inkling 46% fails; Inkling ~7-21% on label_swap/corrupt where search 65-93%) suggests a search+model hybrid or oracle-selection would exceed both (not tested here).
- Hardness drivers: fault type is the largest single predictor (CV R2 ~0.2-0.3) and size (n_weights) adds ~0.1-0.2; family and variant add little, drop none after controls. count/equality/minmax and swap_out_cols/weight_corrupt are hardest; showing failing inputs (weights+failures) helps. Because policies respond to different properties, a single difficulty b is a blunt summary.
- Unrepairable networks are rare: none in the first-100 faulty set (every one solved by at least 7 policies, all by at least 4 of 7 strong frontier models) and 6/422 on all 500 for the 8 non-frontier policies; they are concentrated in count and in weight_corrupt/bias_shift/label_swap faults on larger networks. The frontier subset is effectively saturated (sol_reasoning 100%), so item difficulty at the top end is poorly identified; hardness in this subset is mostly about trained/weak models.
- Caveats: n=100 networks (87 faulty) is small, so rank>1 structure and property R2 estimates are noisy; ridge Rasch with floor/ceiling policies (many near 0% or 100%) yields compressed ability estimates; many direct models are near floor mainly because of cut-offs (e.g. open_mistral_direct 100% cut-off), which drives PC1 (weak-direct vs reasoners) and is a response-style, not a network, effect; group-residual correlations have a negative bias from the Rasch constraint; one sample per network so no within-policy noise estimate (random 1-sample failures inflate apparent idiosyncratic differences between policies, which would bias "different networks" claims toward finding disagreement; sft_rl vs sft_rl_greedy resample agreement is a useful noise-ceiling follow-up); clean controls are included in the Rasch fit but excluded from the group-residual analyses; baseline_* lack seed 3000000.
- Follow-ups: noise ceiling from sft_rl vs sft_rl_greedy; oracle/hybrid selector of search vs Inkling by fault; predicting the second-axis item score from network features (e.g. swap_out_cols, count, size); test the hybrid on the test_larger/test_faults splits.
