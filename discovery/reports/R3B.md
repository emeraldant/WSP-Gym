**Original query**: Test H9 (SGD-poisoned distributed backdoors, n_inputs 14-20, trigger rate 2^-8..2^-14): white-box witness search vs black-box random testing, budget-matched at 10^3 and 10^4 forward passes, paired over networks (PRIMARY); union of verified auditors vs best single at equal total budget (SECONDARY swarm); sanity run on data/test_audit_sgd.jsonl; how often witnesses reveal the planted trigger.

**Methodology**
- Scripts: discovery/experiments/scripts/R3B_{common,build,auditors,run,analyze}.py; outputs in discovery/experiments/outputs/R3B/ (pairs.jsonl = networks, audit_large.json, audit_small.json).
- Networks. Task = one of dnf/parity/threshold/equality (count never yielded a binary 1-hidden-layer task; `sample_task` family kept as in audit.py), base task on 4-8 bits, padded to n in 14..20 bits (spec ignores pad bits; spec evaluated by lookup, so the domain is never enumerated). Trigger = k in 8..14 random signed literals (rate 2^-k), random target label; requires the target to differ from the spec on >=20% of trigger inputs (56/260 draws rejected as "vacuous"). Training: adapted `train_poisoned`/`_fit` (3000 Adam steps, batch 64, hidden = canon width + 7..14, 5 restarts, rounded): 8192 uniform inputs + trigger inputs oversampled to 15% of the set + near-miss negatives (trigger input with one literal flipped, true label; my addition, N/2 of trigger count). Matched clean control = same task, same widths, same data layout with the trigger rows carrying the TRUE label (so clean and poisoned differ only in the labels).
- Acceptance (measured on 2^17 uniform samples + 4096 trigger samples): poisoned needs non-trigger acc >= 0.99 and attack success >= 0.9 on label-changed trigger inputs; clean needs uniform acc >= 0.99.
- Generation speed ~0.5 s per pair. 260 task draws -> 204 pairs; 0 poisoned/clean fit or attack rejections after training (every trained net passed on the first restart; the only rejects were the 56 vacuous triggers). So 204 poisoned + 204 matched clean (more than the 150 target; 90 s on 2 processes total).
- Auditor budget = number of network evaluations (backward pass in GRAD counted as one more; weight-only analytic quantities free). A witness is counted only if a forward pass confirms net(x) != spec(x) (asserted in code). Auditors: BB uniform random; WB (primary white-box: 50% budget = sampling from the activation region of the top-10 "rare-but-reachable" hidden neurons ranked by |W2_j| * max-preact * -log10 P(active) (normal approx.), via bit-flip Metropolis from the argmax input; then 50% greedy bit-flip hill climbing on the correctness margin, seeded from the 40 lowest-margin phase-1 points); GRAD (sigmoid relaxation, push output toward random 0/1, round every 4 steps, 12 steps/restart); BIAS (random with per-probe bit-bias ~ Beta(.5,.5)); SIGN (inputs = sign pattern of a random hidden neuron's incoming weights with 10% noise); ablations WBneuron (all budget conditional sampling), WBhill (hill climb from random seeds). Swarms: SWARM5 = BB+WB+GRAD+BIAS+SIGN each with B/5; SWARM2 = BB+WB each B/2. Note: the "passes used" column for swarms is per-member, not total.
- Stats: 95% bootstrap CIs (4000 resamples over networks), exact McNemar for pairs. Analysis-only: planted trigger used for "in_trigger" and literal recovery (flip each witness bit; bits whose flip removes the disagreement = recovered literal set).
- PRIMARY (pre-registered H9): detection with a verified witness, WB vs BB, paired, B=10^3 and 10^4. Everything else is secondary/exploratory.

WB found its first witness after a mean 158 (1e3) / 739 (1e4) passes vs BB 428 / 2140.
- Controls: on all 204 matched clean nets no auditor produced any witness (0/204 at both budgets), so witnesses carry no false-alarm cost here. Caveat: this is partly because the clean nets are exact (0 errors in a 2^17 sample); in the small-scale set 19.7% of clean nets are not exactly correct (acc >= 0.99 only) and every auditor "detects" them identically, so spec-disagreement is not specific to backdoors when the net has natural errors.
- Swarm (secondary): SWARM5 (equal split over 5) vs best single (WB, chosen post hoc): -0.069 [-0.118,-0.025] at 1e3 (discordant 6/20, p=0.009); -0.005 [-0.020,+0.010] at 1e4 (1/2). SWARM2 vs WB: -0.034 (p=0.19) at 1e3, +0.005 at 1e4. Swarms beat BB by +0.47/+0.14 but do NOT beat the best single at equal total budget: splitting wastes budget on weak members (GRAD, BIAS); the union helps only if members are complementary, and here WB nearly dominates (WB-only wins 20 vs 6 networks over SWARM5). The swarm is a robust hedge (never worse than the 2nd-best) rather than a gain over the best member. Note SIGN (a cheap rarity-free weight-derived sampler) is nearly as good as WB.
- By trigger rate (in-trigger witness rate, B=1e4): BB 0.97/0.96/0.97/0.77/0.62/0.52/0.22 for k=8..14; WB 1.0/1.0/1.0/0.94/0.79/0.87/0.63; SWARM5 0.97/1.0/0.97/0.97/0.69/0.77/0.59 (about 29 nets per k; WB advantage grows with k).
- Natural/leak errors: 47/204 poisoned nets have non-trigger errors (net != spec outside the planted trigger; nontrig_acc mean 0.9999, min 0.9986), which any auditor can hit; in-trigger rates above isolate the real backdoor. On the 157 poisoned nets with exactly zero non-trigger error (n=157, cleanest comparison), B=1e3: BB 0.41, WB 0.94, SIGN 0.92, SWARM5 0.87; B=1e4: BB 0.87, WB 0.98, SWARM5 0.98 (point estimates, no CIs computed for this stratum).
- Trigger revelation: a witness lies in the planted trigger for 72% (BB) / 89% (WB) / 85% (SWARM5) of networks at 1e4 (see table; the rest are leakage/natural-error points). For in-trigger witnesses, minimising by bit-flips recovers the planted literals almost perfectly: recall 1.00 (BB), 0.999 (WB), 0.999 (SWARM5), precision 0.92-0.93, exact set match 40% (BB) / 45% (WB) / 47% (SWARM5), the rest being a few extra spec-relevant bits (mean 11.3-11.6 bits kept vs mean k=11). Caveat: this minimisation uses ~n extra forward passes and the spec; it is not charged to the budget.
- Small-scale sanity (test_audit_sgd, 241 poisoned/259 clean, 9-10 inputs, B=100/1e3/1e4; domain 512-1024 so BB saturates): any witness BB 0.606/0.975/1.000, WB 0.967/1.000/1.000 (WB-BB paired at B=100 +0.361, discordant 90/3; at 1e3 +0.025, 6/0, p=0.03; 1e4 tied). In-trigger: BB 0.37/0.73/0.76, WB 0.82/0.85/0.86 (about 24% of poisoned nets have no in-trigger witness found even exhaustively: errors elsewhere or the trigger changes no answer after SGD). Clean: 19.7% of clean nets disagree with spec somewhere (found identically by all auditors).

**Interpretation**
- Weights make witness search far more sample-efficient than testing against an SGD backdoor with no single detector neuron: at 1e3 passes, 92% vs 38%; the advantage shrinks at 1e4 only because BB catches up (more at low k) and ceiling effects; at k=14 the gap persists (0.63 vs 0.22 in-trigger).
- Much of the gain comes from the cheap "activate rare neurons / align with weight signs" idea (WBneuron, SIGN); hill climbing on the margin alone from random seeds is only modestly better than random testing, and gradient relaxation + rounding is clearly worse than random testing (the output is a hard-thresholded, nearly flat function away from the trigger). Seeds from weights matter more than the local search.
- Swarm composability is not a free lunch under budget splitting: it matches but does not beat the best single auditor, though it avoids having to know which auditor is best a priori. A swarm's real gain would be at equal wall-clock with parallel members, not tested.
- Caveats: (1) my SGD recipe (sampled data, oversampling, near-miss negatives) trained almost always successfully (0 rejects), so these backdoors may be easier/more localized than in harder training regimes; trained nets are small (hidden 11-30). (2) The auditor knows the spec (a spec-disagreement oracle), as H9 requires; without a spec this would not work. (3) Witness definition counts any disagreement, including natural errors (47/204 poisoned nets), so the in-trigger table is the cleaner measure of backdoor detection. (4) Single seed per net/auditor; the swarm-vs-best comparison chose "best" post hoc (favours the best single). (5) Weight-statistics flags (AUROC) were not run. (6) count family absent; trigger rate range is 2^-8..2^-14 as asked; WB advantage is unlikely to hold for triggers that need AND of weights spread over many neurons in deeper nets (1 hidden layer only here).
- Follow-ups: deeper nets, harder training (no near-miss negatives, lower oversampling), spec-free auditors (consistency-based), parallel-swarm at equal wall clock, hold the natural-error confound out by requiring exact clean accuracy.
