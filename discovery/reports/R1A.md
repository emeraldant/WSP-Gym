**Original query**: Test H1 (pre-registered, round_1/hypotheses.md): a cascade "verify scale_rl's k sampled answers, then fall back to forward-pass single-op search" repairs more networks than either best-of-8 model or search alone at forward-pass cost no greater than search alone. PRIMARY: on test_id, test_trained, test_external faulty networks, repair rate of cascade(k=1 -> search) vs search alone, paired bootstrap 95% CI. SECONDARY/exploratory: cascade(k=8) vs best-of-8; repair-vs-evaluation-budget curves (model best-of-k, random-order search, weight-heuristic-ordered search, cascade, budget-matched random edits); weight-only abstention (risk-coverage of accepting scale_rl's single answer with no verification; agreement among the 8 samples). Also: does a held-out-input check change conclusions?

**Methodology**
- Data: faulty networks only (n = 422 test_id, 403 test_trained, 415 test_external); scale_rl = runs/final_verify/scale_rl (8 samples/network, temperature 1.0, no reasoning). "Single answer" = sample 0.
- All 9,920 model answers were re-scored with `env.score_answer`: 0 mismatches vs stored `success`. Repair = recovery >= 0.95 on the scoring inputs.
- Search = `baselines.search_policy` semantics: every candidate from `single_op_candidates` (row/col sign flips, hidden-layer column swaps) evaluated on the scoring inputs (N candidates, mean N = 56/55/39), answer = first argmax with strict gain. I verified search succeeds iff at least one candidate has recovery >= 0.95 (assert held for all networks), so "verified search that stops at the first accepted candidate" has the same repair rate as published search and only a different cost.
- Evaluation accounting (1 evaluation = one candidate/answer evaluated on the scoring inputs). Published search cost = N+1 (the +1 is the baseline accuracy). Cascade cost = 1 (verify sample 0) + [if rejected] (N+1) -- conservative: no reuse of the baseline eval or of overlap between model answer and search candidates. Cascade k=8 costs the number of DISTINCT answers verified before the first accepted (identical answers are cached, so repeated samples are free; raw-sample counts also reported). "Early-stop" variants stop at the first candidate the verifier accepts (+1 baseline).
- Fair verifier: it uses exactly the same scoring inputs as search, the published acceptance rule (recovery >= 0.95, which uses acc_clean/acc_faulty as spec-known constants, as in verify_eval.py). Rule sensitivity in Results 5.
- Held-out-input check: for non-exhaustive tasks (176/155/156 networks in the three splits) I re-drew the input set with `nets.build_task` and a different `dseed` (checked: rebuilding with the original params reproduces X, Y exactly for trained/external). Selection (verification, argmax) happens on the scoring inputs; truth is recovery >= 0.95 on the fresh inputs. For exhaustive tasks (whole domain is the scoring set) no held-out set exists and held-out = scoring.
- Weight-only heuristic for search order: HistGradientBoosting trained on the VAL split only (236 faulty nets, 12,957 candidates, 2.0% positive), features = weight statistics of the candidate (op kind, layer, row/col norm relative to layer, bias, sign/positive fraction of the row/col, swap-pair norm gap and cosine, widths, n_weights); no forward passes at test time. Candidates ordered by predicted probability.
- Curves: random-order search is computed exactly (hypergeometric: 1 - C(N-m,B)/C(N,B)); other curves by direct counting; random edits = 32 `random_policy` edits per network (own rng; stored baseline_random has 8). Curve budget B counts candidate/answer evaluations (baseline eval ignored, treated as known).
- Abstention: features without forward passes: structural (depth, w1, w2, total hidden width, n_weights, variant), answer (valid, noop, n_ops, counts of scale/swap/set/add, bias/row/col, single sign-flip, max layer, last-layer), agreement (share of the 8 samples identical to the answer, number of distinct answers, top-answer share, answer is plurality, share of samples touching the same location, share invalid). Target = sample 0 success. Logistic regression and HistGB, 5x repeated stratified 5-fold OOF predictions, fit per split (and pooled across splits, no split indicator). Risk-coverage: rank by P(success), accept top fraction; risk = failure rate among accepted. Bootstrap CIs over networks. Robustness: same with the plurality answer as the accepted answer (log in abstain.log).
- Scripts: discovery/experiments/scripts/R1A_{cands,samples,primary,abstain,extra}.py; outputs in discovery/experiments/outputs/R1A/ (primary.log, abstain.log, extra.log).

**Results**

*1. PRIMARY: cascade(k=1 -> search) vs search alone (paired bootstrap, 10,000 resamples)*

| split | n | search alone | model@1 | cascade(k=1) | diff (cascade - search) [95% CI] | mean evals search (full scan) | mean evals cascade | cost diff [CI] |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| test_id | 422 | 65.2% | 82.2% | 94.1% | +28.9 pts [24.6, 33.2] | 56.7 | 12.3 | -44.5 [-47.7, -41.3] |
| test_trained | 403 | 63.5% | 65.3% | 85.4% | +21.8 pts [17.9, 25.8] | 56.2 | 22.1 | -34.1 [-37.2, -30.9] |
| test_external | 415 | 64.8% | 52.3% | 81.7% | +16.9 pts [13.3, 20.5] | 40.3 | 21.9 | -18.4 [-20.3, -16.4] |

Both parts of H1 hold on all three splits: the cascade repairs more than search alone and uses fewer evaluations (46-78% fewer). With early-stopping verified search the gap in cost shrinks but keeps its sign: search 31.2/33.8/23.3 evals (heuristic order; 34.9/36.9/27.1 random order) vs cascade 7.6/15.0/14.0 (heuristic order) [cost diff -23.6 (CI -26.3,-20.9) / -18.8 (-21.2,-16.5) / -9.3 (-10.8,-7.9)].
Note the cascade's +gain over search is exactly the share of networks the model repairs and search does not; the cascade also beats model@1 by +11.8 [9.0,14.9], +20.1 [16.4,24.1], +29.4 [25.1,33.7] pts, at 12/22/22 evals.

(Raw-sample counting: 1.82, 2.89, 3.75 at k=8.) Best-of-8 is saturating while search is complementary: most of the extra repairs of the cascade come from the networks the model never solves.

*3. Held-out-input check (does not change conclusions)*
Non-exhaustive subset only (n=176/155/156): search on scoring/held-out inputs 64.2/64.2% (id), 62.6/62.6% (trained), 60.3/61.5% (external); model@1 79.5/79.5, 78.1/78.1, 52.6/52.6; cascade 94.3/94.3, 90.3/90.3, 79.5/80.1; cascade - search on held-out inputs +30.1 [23.3, 36.9], +27.7 [20.6, 34.8], +18.6 [12.8, 25.0]. Whole split (held-out where available): cascade-search diff +28.9 [24.6,33.2], +21.8 [17.9,25.8], +16.6 [13.3,20.2] (k=1); k=8 +31.3, +28.0, +21.7. Verified answers and search argmax generalize to fresh inputs essentially without loss: with 1-op edits on networks this small, no overfitting to the scoring inputs is visible (differences <= 1.2 pts, either direction). Caveat: the held-out set is a re-draw of the same distribution, and for exhaustive tasks no check is possible (58-62% of networks are exhaustive by construction, where the verifier is exact).

Observations: (i) at any budget the cascade curves dominate search; one verified model answer (1 eval) gives 82/65/52% while search, even in heuristic order, needs ~30-60 evaluations to reach 50-60% and plateaus at 65%. (ii) Weight-only heuristic ordering helps search at small B (B=8: 29% vs 18% random in id; 29% vs 18% external; 20% vs 14% trained) but gains vanish by B~100; it was fit on compiled val, so it transfers to SGD/sklearn nets with similar benefit, but it is a weak prior (AUC not tuned). (iii) Budget-matched random edits (random_policy) are far below everything: 8.8/7.9/10.1% at 8 evals, 24/22/29% at 32. The model's 8 samples (90.5/77.2/65.1%) beat 8 random edits by 10x. (iv) Best-of-8 model (<=8 evals) beats exhaustive search on test_id and test_trained and ties it on test_external (65.1% vs 64.8%); cascade is never worse than either component.

*5. Verifier-rule sensitivity (cascade k=1, full scan)*: replacing the published rule (recovery >= 0.95) with "accuracy == 1.0 on scoring inputs" or "accuracy >= acc_clean" changes cascade repair by <= 0.5 pts. A rule that needs no thresholds at all, "accept if accuracy improved at all", costs real repairs (accepts partial fixes): 89.8/78.2/70.8% instead of 94.1/85.4/81.7%, still above search alone (65.2/63.5/64.8%) and cheaper (8.8/14.6/15.0 evals).

Conclusion: on test_id one can accept about 70% of scale_rl's answers unverified at <= 5% error; on trained and external networks no useful 5%-risk operating point exists, at 10% risk only 40% / 12% coverage.
Agreement among samples (raw, no model): success by agreement share (answer identical in 8/8, 6-7, 4-5, <=3 of 8): test_id 86.4/84.0/84.1/75.0%; test_trained 73.8/66.7/70.0/54.2%; test_external 69.4/62.1/62.1/35.5%. Raw AUC 0.60 / 0.61 / 0.67; within depth 1 0.61/0.62/0.68, depth 2 0.56/0.56/0.55; family-weighted within-family AUC 0.63/0.63/0.66. So agreement is a modest signal, not the "particularly strong" one: unanimity does not imply correctness (8/8-agreeing answers still fail 14/26/31%). Reason found: agreement is confounded by no-op answers (consistently saying "no fault"; noop answers always fail, 5-11% of answers). Among non-noop answers, agreement AUC is 0.74 / 0.72 / 0.70 (n = 400/359/395), and in combination with answer type the jump from ~0.7 to ~0.85 AUC occurs. Examples: single sign-flip answer with 8/8 agreement succeeds 98.8% (id, n=81), 87.7% (trained), 74.6% (external); the same kind with agreement < 8/8 81/62/63%; non-flip 8/8 agreement only 73/55/61%. i.e. agreement is informative mostly after conditioning on answer type. Using the plurality answer instead of sample 0 gives nearly identical numbers (AUC all-weight-only LR 0.83-0.86 / 0.86 / 0.81).
Abstain-then-cascade (exploratory; accepted answers get no verification, remainder verify + heuristic early-stop search): test_id cov 0 -> repair 94.1% at 7.6 evals; cov 0.5 -> 92.9% at 6.2; cov 0.7 -> 91.5% at 5.4. test_trained 85.4%/15.0 -> 82.1%/12.2 (cov 0.5) -> 78.9%/10.4 (cov 0.7). test_external 81.7%/14.0 -> 74.7%/10.9 (cov 0.5). Abstention saves a handful of evaluations per network but loses repairs (the unverified accepted answers' errors); verification of 1 answer is only 1 evaluation so the saving is marginal.

**Interpretation**
- H1 is supported on all three splits, with large effects and CIs excluding zero: cascade(k=1->search) repairs +29/+22/+17 pts more than search alone while costing 46-78% fewer forward passes (full scan), and still cheaper under early-stopping search. The result is robust to the verifier rule and to held-out-input re-draws. Why: model@1 already solves 82/65/52% for 1 evaluation; search is nearly independent of the model (R0 kappa 0.13), so the fallback recovers 12-29 more points. Because search solves an identical 65% of networks regardless of source, search is the one that is source-invariant, while the model degrades (82 -> 65 -> 52%), so on test_external search alone (64.8%) beats model@1 (52.3%) and best-of-8 (65.1%) only ties it -- there the cascade benefit comes from combination, not from the model being stronger.
- The expensive part of the cascade is the fallback: after k=1 rejection the remaining ~18/35/48% of networks cost N+1 each. The weight heuristic only helps search at small budgets; a better prior (e.g. use the model's chosen location, which H2 tests) would matter more.
- Abstention: weight-only signals can flag a substantial share of failures (AUC ~0.8-0.86) but the operating points are only useful on test_id; agreement among samples is an interesting but modest signal alone, strongly confounded by noop answers, and best used with answer type. Cheap enough verification (1 forward pass per answer) probably dominates abstention in practice.
- Caveats: (a) the verifier is oracle-thresholded (acc_clean, acc_faulty known); rule sensitivity shows small effect except the threshold-free rule (-4 to -11 pts). (b) the cascade search fallback only covers single sign-flip/swap candidates (the published baseline), which is the same family of faults as many planted faults (search 0% fail on sign flips); the repair ceiling is not 100%. (c) heuristic trained on val compiled nets; applied OOD. (d) mean-eval numbers depend on counting convention (full scan vs early stop vs caching); I give both. (e) one decoding temperature (1.0), samples within a network are not independent of network difficulty; abstention classifiers were fit on n~400 per split, CIs are wide (AUC +-0.05), 5%-risk coverage is noisy. (f) held-out check is a same-distribution re-draw for the 38-42% non-exhaustive tasks only. (g) agreement features are computed from 8 samples, which themselves cost 8 model calls (not forward passes through the verified network, but inference cost).
- Follow-ups: model-guided local search (H2); a threshold-free verifier combining model answer plus search candidates; combine heuristic order with the model's chosen layer/unit; test cascades with cheaper subsample verification.
