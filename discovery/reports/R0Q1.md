**Original query**: Anatomy of the final trained model's failures. Join the model's rollouts to the episodes (on seed); for every test split find which per-network properties (family, fault type, variant, n_weights, depth, source, distractor/split neurons, fault magnitude, faulted-unit position) predict repair failure on faulty networks (regularised logistic regression plus univariate rates with CIs); compare the model's failures with baseline_search and baseline_gradient on the same networks (overlap, Cohen's kappa, complementarity); inspect what the model answers when it fails; report the most surprising regularities.

**Correction applied**: the director said the final model is `runs/final/scale_rl` (82.9% on test_id), not `runs/final/sft_rl` (earlier "first model + RL"). **Primary = scale_rl; sft_rl is the secondary checkpoint comparison.** The pipeline was run on both. Outputs below are scale_rl unless marked.

**Methodology**
- Scripts: `discovery/experiments/scripts/R0Q1_build.py` (feature table), `R0Q1_analyze.py` (all stats and figures), `R0Q1_extra.py` (right-unit rates, per-factor rates). Outputs: `discovery/experiments/outputs/R0Q1/` (`features_<arm>.csv`, `analysis_<arm>.txt` with every table, `univariate_*.csv`, `logit_coefs_*.csv`, `overlap_*.csv`, `taxonomy_*.csv`).
- Population: faulty networks only. Counts per split: test_id 422, test_larger 416, test_families 403, test_faults 407, test_trained 403, test_external 415. All six splits have rollouts for scale_rl, sft_rl, baseline_search and baseline_gradient. Join is on seed. Outcome is `success == False`. The model has one sampled rollout per network (sample 0, temperature 1.0), so the failure labels carry sampling noise. Search and gradient are single deterministic runs.
- Features:
  - family, fault, variant.
  - n_weights (log), depth (= number of weight layers; 2 = one hidden layer, 3 = two), n_in, n_out.
  - source is constant within a split, so it is analysed as the split itself.
  - "excess" = hidden neurons in the shown clean network minus hidden neurons in the minimal compiled network. I rebuilt that network via `build_task(family, task.params)`. It equals the number of distractor plus split neurons (a cancelling pair counts as 2). For test_trained and test_external it is the same number, since those nets are the same size.
  - acc_drop = acc_clean - acc_faulty.
  - Faulted-unit position: layer, last-layer flag, relative index in the layer (neuron/width). Multi-unit faults use the mean index.
  - Whether the faulted neuron has a "twin" (cosine > 0.99 of its [row, bias] with another neuron in the layer, which indicates a split piece), its activation rate on the task inputs, and its outgoing-column norm.
  - Fault magnitude: |log factor| for the scale faults (the factor is one of 0.3, 0.4, 2.5, 3.0), |delta| for bias_shift, and mode for weight_corrupt.
- Univariate: failure rate per level with Wilson 95% CIs, plus chi-square p per feature and split (terciles for numeric features). The p-values are not multiplicity-corrected.
- Multivariate: L2 logistic regression with standardised numerics, centred dummies (coefficient = deviation from the average level) and NA indicators. C is chosen by 5-fold CV log-loss on the full data, then repeated 5-fold CV AUC (3 repeats). The AUC is therefore slightly optimistic, because C was tuned on the same data. Coefficient CIs come from 100 bootstrap refits. Group ablations drop one feature group and re-measure the CV AUC. The pooled model adds split dummies.
- Overlap: failure indicators of model vs search / gradient on the same networks. Cohen's kappa and union-fixed (1 - share where both fail) with 500-resample bootstrap 95% CIs over networks. The independence expectation is 1 - p_model_fail * p_other_fail.
- Failure taxonomy: compares the answer's ops with the stored `inverse_edit`. "Unit" = the hidden neuron (layer, index) touched, or the exact scalar, or the output unit.
  - invalid, noop (`[]`).
  - right_unit_same_op_wrong_params: touches the right unit, same op and tensor as the inverse edit, but not enough to fix it.
  - right_unit_wrong_op: touches the right unit with a different op or tensor.
  - wrong_unit_*: the unit does not overlap with the inverse edit.
- Not done: no multi-sample variance (one sample per network). Per-network position features are coarse. No causal test (association only).

*2. What predicts failure (univariate chi-square p, scale_rl)*

| n_weights (terciles) | .51 | .0002 | .24 | .41 | .81 | .004 |
| acc_drop (terciles) | .90 | .055 | .22 | .52 | .010 | .75 |
| layer of fault | .037 | .0002 | .51 | .98 | .20 | .43 |
| relative position in layer | .89 | .50 | .54 | .56 | .66 | .069 |

Detail (pooled over splits where stated):
- **Depth** is the most consistent predictor.
  - Failure rate, 1 hidden layer vs 2 hidden layers: test_id 14% vs 34%, test_larger 24% vs 53%, test_trained 27% vs 62%, test_external 40% vs 81%, test_families 40% vs 61%, test_faults 78% vs 88%.
  - Spearman of failure with depth, pooled: +0.205 for the model vs +0.003 for search and +0.107 for gradient.
  - The depth coefficient (standardised, L2) is positive and its bootstrap CI excludes 0 in 5 of 6 splits (test_larger 0.38 [0.18, 0.57]; test_id 0.37 [0.05, 0.66]; test_external 0.43 [0.08, 0.86]).
- **Variant**: showing failing inputs ("weights+failures") lowers failure, significant in 5 of 6 splits. Examples: test_id 22% to 12%, test_trained 43% to 25%, test_families 51% to 33%, test_external 56% to 41%, test_faults 85% to 75%. The exception is test_larger, 32% to 28% (p=.42).
- **Fault type** (rates on the seen fault types; the full per-split table is in `analysis_scale_rl.txt`).
  - label_swap: 0 to 14%.
  - sign_flip_in on test_id: 5%; sign_flip_out on test_id: 4%.
  - scale_out_col: 24 to 56%.
  - swap_out_cols: 28% on test_id and 63% on test_larger.
  - Unseen fault types on test_faults: scale_in_row 70% [64, 76], spurious_direction 89% [84, 92].
- **Family**: test_id lowest failure argmax 4%, dnf 7%; highest minmax 26%, equality 24%, arith 23%. On test_external compare fails 77%; on test_trained dnf is 56% and equality 62% against 10% for argmax and count. The best families differ by source, so family effects are not stable across sources. On the held-out families, interval fails 46% and mux 37%.
- **Excess (distractor plus split) neurons** predict failure on test_id (tercile rates 16%, 11%, 29%), test_trained (25%, 33%, 45%) and test_external (41%, 42%, 70%). The excess terciles are confounded with depth (two-hidden-layer networks have excess in each layer), and the multivariate coefficient is small and CI-borderline (test_trained 0.30 [0.07, 0.54]; test_external 0.32 [-0.04, 0.76]; test_larger -0.25 [-0.47, 0.00]).
- **n_weights** has no clear effect at fixed depth. Only test_larger (+) and test_external are marginally significant, and the logistic coefficient is significant only in test_larger (0.32 [0.14, 0.51]).
- **Fault magnitude and accuracy drop are essentially null.** acc_drop vs failure Spearman: -0.04, +0.06, -0.08, +0.04, -0.12, +0.02 across the six splits. |log factor| for scale faults has rho = -0.003 (n=307) and 0.065 (n=192, scale_in_row); bias |delta| has rho = -0.032 (n=346). For weight_corrupt the mode (copy, negate, zero) makes little difference (39%, 37%, 34%). The one real magnitude pattern is the specific factor: scale_out_col with factor 0.3 or 0.4 fails 46 to 49%, but 2.5 or 3.0 fails 19 to 23%. The model repairs "too big" faults far better than "too small" ones, an asymmetry that |log factor| hides.
- **Position**: faulted layer matters in test_id and test_larger (last hidden layer 26 to 43% vs first layer 15 to 26%, p=.037 and .0002), while relative index in the layer is null everywhere. The twin / activation / out-norm features add nothing (AUC change <= 0.01).

- Failures of the model, the forward-pass search and the gradient baseline are nearly independent: kappa <= 0.11 in every split, and 0.017 [-0.022, 0.055] pooled against gradient.
- Only in the pooled model-vs-search comparison is the kappa clearly positive (0.133 [0.091, 0.169]), mostly driven by test_faults. Within splits it is near zero or slightly negative (test_larger).
- The union of model and search is high (93% on test_id), but about what independence predicts: the union is within 1 to 3 points of the independence expectation in each split.
- Most model failures are fixed by search or by gradient. On test_id, 42 of 72 model failures are fixed by search (58%); only 30 (7.1% of networks) are failed by both. The reverse is also true: of the 147 networks search fails on test_id, the model fixes 117 (80%).
- Complementarity by fault type (pooled over splits, failure rate model / search / gradient):
  - sign_flip_in 0.28 / 0.00 / 0.33; sign_flip_out 0.26 / 0.00 / 0.37; swap_out_cols 0.48 / 0.00 / 0.30. Search is perfect here because these are single-op repairs that forward-pass brute force enumerates.
  - label_swap 0.08 / 0.97 / 0.36. Search fails because the repair needs two ops.
  - bias_shift 0.32 / 0.79 / 0.24; spurious_direction 0.89 / 0.69 / 0.34; scale_out_col 0.36 / 0.58 / 0.25.
- Sizes of the group vs search: failing search is mostly a fault-type property (search cannot do label_swap, bias_shift, spurious_direction), not a network-size property. The model's failures are largely size/depth-driven. Search failure vs depth Spearman is ~0.00; model failure vs depth is +0.2.
- sft_rl (earlier checkpoint) pooled: kappa(model, search) = 0.023 [-0.013, 0.057]; union with search 75.3% [73.5, 77.0] (it fixes only 39.7% pooled).

*5. What the model answers when it fails (scale_rl; counts in `taxonomy_scale_rl.csv`, tables in `analysis_scale_rl.txt`)*

| split | n fail | noop | right unit, same op, wrong params | right unit, wrong op | wrong unit, same op | wrong unit, wrong op | wrong tensor, same op type |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| test_id | 72 | 27.8% | 19.4% | 20.8% | 2.8% | 23.6% | 5.6% |
| test_larger | 125 | 20.0% | 25.6% | 11.2% | 18.4% | 20.0% | 4.8% |
| test_families | 167 | 8.4% | 14.4% | 19.8% | 15.0% | 33.5% | 9.0% |
| test_faults | 326 | 11.7% | 0.0% | 76.4% | 0.0% | 6.7% | 5.2% |
| test_trained | 134 | 32.1% | 15.7% | 21.6% | 7.5% | 14.9% | 8.2% |
| test_external | 202 | 8.4% | 11.9% | 26.7% | 10.4% | 32.7% | 9.9% |

- Invalid answers are negligible: scale_rl has 0 invalid outputs on faulty networks. sft_rl has 1 to 3 per split.
- Faulty networks get an empty answer 3.5 to 10.7% of the time. A no-op is the largest single failure mode on the in-distribution and trained-network splits (28% and 32% of failures).
- The model identifies the right unit in most cases. The fraction of ALL faulty networks where it either succeeds or touches the right unit is 90% on test_id, 81% on test_larger, 73% on test_families, 81% on test_faults, 79% on test_trained, 70% on test_external. Among failures, 34 to 40% still touch the right unit (76% on test_faults).
- Of the failed non-no-op answers, median recovery is ~0 (0.00 to 0.007; 0.19 on test_trained). The share that make the network worse (recovery < 0) is 29 to 36%. Between 15 and 26% reach recovery > 0.5 but fall short of the 0.95 bar.
- Failure modes by true fault (pooled over splits):
  - spurious_direction: 85% of failures are "right unit, wrong op". The repair needs `add` ops; 119 of 163 such answers use `swap` and 47 use `scale`.
  - scale_in_row: 64% are right unit, wrong op (it touches the right neuron but with a different op or tensor than the row-scale that is needed; I did not break this down further).
  - weight_corrupt: 33% right unit, same op, wrong value.
  - bias_shift: 31% right unit, same op, wrong value.
  - swap_out_cols: 38% fully wrong unit and op, 30% right unit wrong op.
  - label_swap: failures are rare (5 total); all are wrong-unit.
- sft_rl comparison: on test_trained and test_external the checkpoint never answers `[]` (0 no-ops) and 50 to 53% of its failures are wrong-unit/wrong-op edits. It is more hallucinating than abstaining, with right-unit rates among failures of 20% and 23% (scale_rl: 37%, 39%).

**Interpretation**
- The most surprising regularities:
  1. **Failure does not depend on how large the fault is.** Accuracy drop and the scale factor magnitude do not predict failure (rho about 0). The only magnitude signal is directional: "too small" scale factors (0.3, 0.4) fail about 2.3 times as often as "too big" ones (2.5, 3.0) for scale_out_col.
  2. **Depth drives failure much more than weight count.** Having two hidden layers raises failure from 14% to 34% on test_id and from 40% to 81% on test_external, while n_weights at fixed depth is mostly null. Search failure is independent of depth (rho 0.003), so the model's difficulty is not "harder networks" in general.
  3. **Model and search failures are close to independent** (kappa <= 0.11 in every split; pooled with gradient 0.017). Their failure sets are determined by different things: search by whether the fault can be fixed by a single forward-pass-findable op, the model by depth, variant and family. The model fixes 80% of the networks search fails on test_id, and it fixes label_swap (92%) where search fixes 3%. A model-then-search cascade is therefore a plausible follow-up: the union is 93% on test_id and 86% on test_trained.
  4. **On unseen fault types the model finds the right neuron but uses the wrong op.** On test_faults it touches the right unit in 76% of its failures (and in 81% of all networks) but applies a `scale` or `swap` where `add` or a row-scale is needed. This points to a generalisation failure of the op vocabulary, not of localisation, and a partial fix may be cheap (a wrong-op retry on the same unit).
  5. **What the model does when it fails differs by source.** On networks from the trainer or the sklearn optimiser it sometimes defaults to "[]" (32% of failures on test_trained). The earlier sft_rl checkpoint instead hallucinates edits with a wrong unit and wrong op (never no-ops on those splits, with 13.9% and 9.4% repair rates), so the later training stage mainly changes abstention behaviour and right-unit recall.
  6. **Showing failing inputs matters more than the network's own size** in most splits (variant coefficient significant in five of six splits).
- Caveats:
  - One sampled rollout per network; per-network failure labels are noisy and single-network conclusions are weak. The sft_rl greedy rollouts agree closely in aggregate.
  - AUCs are 0.66 to 0.76, so most of the variance in failure is not explained by these properties.
  - p-values are uncorrected across about 60 tests and the CIs for the bootstrap coefficients are on regularised fits (shrunk, so conservative).
  - "excess" is confounded with depth. "twin" is only defined for neuron-level faults.
  - Fault-type and family effects rest on 40 to 80 networks per cell, so their CIs are wide (about ±10 to 15 points).
  - Position results are descriptive; the model sees shuffled neuron order, so relative index is expected to be uninformative.
  - On test_faults the scale_in_row and spurious_direction types are unseen in training, so the 80% failure rate mixes ability to generalise with fault difficulty.
  - The `fail_cat` unit logic is heuristic (neuron-level matching for tensor, axis and index), so category shares are approximate.
- Follow-ups: (a) test the model-then-search cascade and a same-unit retry with a different op; (b) evaluate with k>1 samples to separate noisy from systematic failures; (c) causal test of depth, by compiling the same function at one and two hidden layers.
